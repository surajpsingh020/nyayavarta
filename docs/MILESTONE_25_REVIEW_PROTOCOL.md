# NyayaVarta — Independent Legal Review Protocol (Milestone 25)

## 1. Purpose and Scope of Review

This protocol governs the independent legal evaluation of claim-level verification and citation attribution in **NyayaVarta**, an open-source Indian legal retrieval and question-answering system.

### Scope of the Reviewer's Mandate
- **Isolated Claim-Evidence Evaluation**: You are evaluating whether specific substantive legal propositions generated by an automated system are faithfully grounded in, and supported by, specific cited excerpts from judgments of the Supreme Court of India.
- **No System-Level Safety Certification**: You are **not** being asked to certify that NyayaVarta is "safe for production", "legally infallible", or ready for public deployment. Your evaluation is strictly confined to the 30 blinded cases in the evaluation pack.
- **Blinded Evaluation**: All machine-generated confidence scores, internal verifier statuses, and developer labels have been removed from the reviewer template to guarantee impartial adjudication.

---

## 2. Primary Annotation Outcomes

For each blinded case in `evaluation/rag/milestone25_reviewer_template.jsonl`, assign exactly one of the following five primary outcomes:

### 1. `SUPPORTED`
- **Definition**: The cited excerpt (read alongside the surrounding context) directly, affirmatively, and adequately establishes the proposition exactly as formulated.
- **Criteria**:
  - The core doctrine or factual statement is faithfully reflected in the court's reasoning.
  - There are no omitted mandatory statutory conditions, prerequisites, or procedural caveats that change the legal effect of the proposition.
  - The passage reflects an operative judicial finding or ratio decidendi of the bench.

### 2. `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`
- **Definition**: The cited evidence supports only a qualified, conditional, or narrower version of the proposition.
- **Criteria**:
  - The proposition states a general rule, but the court established that the rule is subject to mandatory prerequisites (e.g., written consent, prior sanction, functionality test, or factual determination on remand).
  - The proposition asserts affirmative power, but the court held that such power is discretionary and confined to exceptional circumstances (e.g., Article 142 equitable relief).
  - A multi-part proposition where one part is supported by the passage, but a second conjoined assertion is absent or ungrounded.

### 3. `UNSUPPORTED`
- **Definition**: The cited evidence does not adequately establish the proposition.
- **Criteria**:
  - The cited passage deals with a completely different subject matter (e.g., an excerpt on CGST tax exemptions cited for anticipatory bail under PMLA).
  - The proposition asserts a specific factual finding or legal holding that cannot be reasonably inferred from the excerpt.
  - The citation marker points to a valid judgment, but the specific proposition is an ungrounded hallucination.

### 4. `CONTRADICTED`
- **Definition**: The cited evidence directly conflicts with, negates, or reverses the proposition.
- **Criteria**:
  - **Holding Inversion**: The claim asserts the court held $X$, whereas the court explicitly rejected $X$ or affirmed $\neg X$.
  - **Polarity Inversion**: The claim asserts a court "must routinely quash" or "cannot order", whereas the cited passage holds the exact opposite.
  - **Doctrinal Conflict**: The claim asserts a precedent was overruled when the cited judgment explicitly reaffirmed it (e.g., asserting Kesavananda Bharati was overruled).

### 5. `AMBIGUOUS`
- **Definition**: The available material is genuinely indeterminate, permits materially conflicting legal interpretations, or the provided excerpt is insufficient to determine the ratio decidendi without broader context.
- **Criteria**:
  - The legal doctrine itself is in flux or the subject of conflicting division bench opinions not resolved by the excerpt.
  - The language of the court is open to two equally plausible doctrinal constructions.
  - The excerpt leaves it uncertain whether an observation was intended as binding ratio or obiter dictum.

## 3. Source Role Classification

To detect structural attribution errors (such as mistaking counsel's submissions for the court's judgment), record the role of the cited passage using one of these categories:

| Source Role | Definition | Example / Indicator |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `court_holding` | Operative ratio decidendi, definitive determination, or judicial finding of the bench. | Prefaced by "Held:", "We find that", "In our considered opinion", or appearing in headnote holdings. |
| `party_submission` | Contentions, arguments, or submissions of counsel or parties recorded by the court. | Prefaced by "Mr. Senior Counsel contended", "Learned ASG submitted", or "The appellant urged". |
| `quoted_precedent` | Excerpt from an earlier decision cited as historical background or persuasive reference. | Contains historical case citations (e.g., "As held in Maneka Gandhi (supra)"). |
| `facts_procedural` | Narration of background facts, trial chronology, or issue framing. | Recital of FIR dates, trial court orders, or framing of reference questions. |
| `statutory_text` | Verbatim reproduction of legislative enactments, sections, rules, or notifications. | "Section 17(5)(d) reads as follows: ...". |
| `other` | Concurring observations, separate opinions, obiter dicta, or editorial headnote text. | Editorial catchwords or general philosophical observations. |

## 4. Guidelines for Detailed Fields

### A. Missing Qualifications or Conditions (`missing_qualifications_or_conditions`)
If you assign `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`, explicitly state what qualification or prerequisite was omitted.
- *Example*: "The claim asserts that auction purchasers may deposit balance consideration after 15 days unconditionally, but Rule 9(4) explicitly requires the written consent of both parties."
- *Example*: "The claim asserts shopping malls qualify as plant under Section 17(5)(d), but the court remanded the matter for factual satisfaction of the functionality test."

### B. Requesting Extended Context (`requires_more_context` & `context_request_details`)
If the provided 350-character excerpt and surrounding context do not allow you to definitively determine whether the passage represents the court's holding or counsel's submission:
1. Set `requires_more_context: true`.
2. In `context_request_details`, state what additional context is needed (e.g., "Need full paragraph containing paras 25–28 to verify whether the court adopted counsel's argument").
3. Assign your provisional verdict or mark `AMBIGUOUS` with a note explaining the limitation.

### C. Reviewer Confidence (`reviewer_confidence`)
- `high`: You are certain of the classification based on settled Supreme Court jurisprudence and clear textual evidence.
- `medium`: The classification is likely correct, but reasonable legal minds might debate the boundary between partial support and ambiguous obiter.
- `low`: The excerpt is fragmentary, or the doctrine is subject to complex statutory nuances requiring inspection of the full unedited law report.

---

## 5. Reviewer Ethical Commitments & Blinding Integrity

1. **Strict Blinding**: Do not attempt to de-anonymize system predictions or consult the development team regarding internal model classifications until all 30 annotations have been submitted.
2. **Neutral Adjudication**: Evaluate claims solely on legal merit against the cited Supreme Court record, without making allowances for AI limitations.
3. **No Fabrication**: If an annotation form is incomplete or skipped, leave it explicitly null; never interpolate or simulate annotations.
